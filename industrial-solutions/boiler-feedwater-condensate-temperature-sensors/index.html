Boiler Feedwater & Condensate Temperature Sensors

Boiler feedwater and condensate temperature sensors are selected for the actual water-system point, connection and signal path. These measurements should be separated from hotter gas or steam duties.

Start with the point: feedwater, condensate or another identified auxiliary location. Record the line or vessel arrangement, normal and upset conditions, connection, insertion, accessibility, response, cable route and receiving instrument. Explain whether the reading is for control, monitoring or equipment protection.

RTD With Weatherproof Head and Two-Wire Loop-Power Temperature Transmitter for Field Mounting are listed catalogue options for review. A head-type RTD and a field transmitter address different parts of the measurement chain. The boiler application guidance also discusses RTDs for moderate-temperature points, but the product record must determine the available assembly.

Confirm RTD details, head, dimensions, cable, connection and transmitter input from the relevant records. Do not infer pressure, material, temperature limits or installation requirements. Include the available clearance and maintenance approach so the proposed sensor is considered for the real feedwater or condensate point.

Share the piping or equipment sketch, conditions and signal requirement with Yog Electro Process. A clear point description lets the team discuss the supported RTD and transmitter options for the boiler auxiliary system.

Feedwater and condensate enquiries should identify the line or equipment point, the available connection and the monitoring channel. Ask whether the RTD is direct-wired or paired with a field transmitter, and state the insertion and maintenance access. Include normal and upset conditions supplied by the project. This gives the team enough context to compare a weatherproof-head RTD with the two-wire transmitter record while keeping the selection tied to the actual boiler auxiliary point.

Include the feedwater or condensate line sketch and instrument list in the request. Identify the exact point and access for replacement, then state whether a direct RTD or transmitter output is preferred. This keeps the catalogue review practical and avoids treating all boiler auxiliary points as identical.

State the desired reading unit, channel arrangement and connector standard where known. Clarify whether the assembly remains installed or is changed during boiler service. This helps keep the selected option aligned with the water-system method, wiring and maintenance routine.

Include the project point list, drawing references and instrument details so the buyer and engineering team discuss the same measurement arrangement.

Keep the requested point, connection and channel together in the enquiry so the selected option remains traceable to the boiler auxiliary measurement.
