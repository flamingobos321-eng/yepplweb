Temperature Sensors for Automotive Heat-Treatment Furnaces

Automotive heat-treatment furnace temperature sensors are chosen by furnace zone, thermal cycle, atmosphere, mounting and the control or recording system receiving the signal.

Separate furnace control, monitoring and profiling points before selecting an assembly. Record the zone, normal and upset conditions, atmosphere, cycling, insertion, access and cable route. A point used for control may have a different construction from one used to document a thermal cycle.

Thermocouple MI is listed for ovens, kilns and furnaces, with J and K selectable types, grounded or ungrounded junctions and several sheath options. R Type Thermocouple is another catalogue option for furnace and high-temperature measurement. Review these records against the specific point rather than treating one family as universal.

Confirm thermocouple type, junction, sheath, dimensions, cable and mounting with the receiving instrument and furnace layout. Consider replacement access and the way the junction is positioned in the zone. The application guide identifies these as selection decisions, while the product record supplies the available construction choices.

For a furnace enquiry, share the zone description, thermal cycle, atmosphere, insertion and instrument input. Yog Electro Process can help narrow the catalogue-backed options for the actual automotive heat-treatment arrangement.

A furnace point schedule should identify each zone and its role in the thermal cycle. Ask where the junction sits, how much clearance exists, whether the instrument expects J, K or R type, and whether grounded or ungrounded construction is required. Also describe access for inspection and replacement. This information helps keep the catalogue choice aligned with the furnace layout and avoids treating a general heat-treatment description as a complete specification.

Include the furnace drawing, control channel and replacement method with the request. State the required thermocouple type and junction if already defined, then ask the team to compare the available product records. A focused request helps buyers evaluate the MI and R Type options according to the real furnace zone, mounting space and maintenance approach.

State the desired reading unit, channel arrangement and connector standard where known. Clarify whether the assembly is changed between furnace cycles or remains installed. This helps keep the selected option aligned with the furnace control method and maintenance routine.

Include the project point list, drawing references and instrument details so the buyer and engineering team discuss the same measurement arrangement.
