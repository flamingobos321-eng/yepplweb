Engine Lube Oil Temperature Measurement

Engine lube oil temperature measurement connects an oil-system point to the test instrument used to record engine behaviour. Sensor choice depends on the actual point, fitting and test arrangement.

Define whether the sensor measures oil in a line, housing or another identified point. Note the connection, available insertion, oil path, expected conditions, vibration, cable route and acquisition channel. The same engine may require different assemblies at different points, so the location should be named in the enquiry.

The catalogue includes Engine Lube Oil Temperature Sensor and Thermocouple With Threaded Fixed Plug. The latter is listed for pipe, tank and vessel measurement and can be reviewed when a threaded process connection is part of the point. The product record, not a broad engine description, determines available types, sheath and cable options.

Selection should also cover junction construction, lead protection, access for replacement and the instrument input. Keep the sensor geometry and installed location consistent when comparing test results. Do not infer a pressure, material or temperature limit from the application name.

Send the oil-system drawing, connection details, channel type and test conditions when requesting a quotation. Yog Electro Process can then discuss the catalogue-backed options that correspond to the actual lube-oil measurement point.

For oil work, the measurement point should be described in enough detail to distinguish a line location from a housing or other engine point. State the available thread or fitting, insertion space, oil-system access and whether the test uses a direct thermocouple or another input. Ask how the lead will be routed around the rig and how replacement will be performed. These practical details make comparison between the listed engine-oil and threaded options more useful.

Include the engine model or test-rig reference only as context; the actual measurement point remains the key selection input. State the oil path, connection, insertion and lead termination, then identify the instrument channel. This keeps the discussion focused on the catalogue-backed product options and the physical arrangement rather than implying that one sensor fits every engine test.

State the desired reading unit, sampling arrangement and connector standard where known. Clarify whether the sensor is installed for one test or repeated engine changes. This helps keep the selected catalogue option aligned with the test method, wiring and maintenance routine.

Include the project point list, drawing references and instrument details so the buyer and engineering team discuss the same measurement arrangement.
