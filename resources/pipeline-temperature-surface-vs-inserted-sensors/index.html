Pipeline Temperature Measurement: Surface vs Inserted Sensors

Pipe-surface and inserted process sensors answer different measurement questions and should be compared by measurement point and mounting. The distinction helps specification teams ask for the appropriate assembly.

Start by deciding whether the required value is the pipe surface temperature or the temperature at a process point inside the line. Surface and process temperatures can differ with the pipe, insulation, ambient conditions, flow and installation. Ask where the point sits, what access is available, whether the line can accept the intended mounting, and how the sensor signal will reach the instrument. Also describe the required response and the method used to check the reading. Note whether the line is accessible from outside and whether the point must remain measurable during maintenance.

The catalogue includes a curved-plate Thermocouple for Pipe Temp Measurement. Its applications include pipeline temperature measurement, surface temperature measurement, pipe, tank and vessel measurement, and engine test temperature measurement. The record lists K, J, T and RTD choices, grounded or ungrounded (isolated) junctions, and SS304 or SS316 sheath options. Dimensions are listed as according to the requirement. The curved form should be compared with the pipe geometry and available contact area.

A Jubilee-clip surface thermocouple is another catalogue-backed option for pipe and surface measurement. Its applications include surface temperature measurement, pipe, tank and vessel measurement, and pipeline surface temperature measurement. The record lists J and K, grounded or ungrounded junctions, Mild Steel, SS304 or SS316 sheath options, and a Jubilee clip mounting approach. A threaded fixed-plug thermocouple is listed for pipe, tank and vessel measurement and should be considered when a threaded process connection is the intended arrangement. These mounting approaches answer different access and installation questions.

Selection should keep the measurement objective separate from assumptions about the process boundary. Provide pipe location, surface access, process conditions, mounting space, response requirement, cable route and instrument input. If an inserted assembly is being considered, include the connection and dimensions needed for the project review. State whether the requested value is surface or process temperature and how the reading will be used. An enquiry with these details allows the relevant catalogue records and their listed options to be compared. Include the existing sensor type and termination when the request concerns replacement. A clear pipe sketch can also show contact position, access direction and available cable space.
