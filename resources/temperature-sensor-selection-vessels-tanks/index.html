Temperature Sensor Selection for Process Vessels & Storage Tanks

Vessel and tank temperature measurement requires the sensing assembly, process connection and signal to be considered together. Clear point information makes catalogue comparison more useful.

Define the measurement point before choosing a sensor. Record the medium, normal and upset conditions, location, access, connection, insertion, response requirement, maintenance method and receiving instrument. Ask whether the point is intended to represent the vessel contents, a tank zone, a wall or a connected line. The answer affects the mounting arrangement and the type of assembly that should be reviewed. Also identify whether the vessel or tank drawing has a suitable existing connection and where the cable can leave the assembly.

The catalogue includes a Thermocouple With Threaded Fixed Plug for pipe, tank and vessel measurement. Its listed types are J and K, with grounded or ungrounded (isolated) junction choices, SS304 or SS316 sheath options, and listed sheath diameters of 4.7 mm, 6.35 mm and 8 mm, among other required dimensions. A threaded arrangement should be considered only when it matches the available connection and project details. The selected insertion and sheath dimensions must be compared with the point drawing.

The catalogue also includes a flameproof thermocouple with flange and an RTD with Weatherproof Head. These are distinct catalogue constructions, so each record should be reviewed for the options it lists. The application plan identifies threaded probes, flange thermocouples, weatherproof-head RTDs and field-mounted transmitters as relevant product families for vessel and tank selection. A field transmitter may be considered when the signal interface requires it, with its own catalogue record checked separately. Do not transfer a connection, material or dimension from one product record to another.

Safe selection is a comparison exercise, not a choice based on vessel or tank name alone. Provide the medium, point location, connection, insertion, access, cable route, response objective and instrument input. Include the drawing information needed to review the assembly and the maintenance method for removal or replacement. An enquiry with these details makes it possible to compare the threaded, flange, RTD and transmitter records that fit the stated measurement question. Explain whether the reading is intended for control, indication, testing or equipment monitoring. Include the existing connection and sensor termination when replacing an assembly. Identify the number of points and whether each location uses the same connection and insertion.
