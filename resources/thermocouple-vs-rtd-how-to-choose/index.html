Thermocouple vs RTD: How to Choose for an Industrial Application

Thermocouples and RTDs both measure temperature, but the right choice starts with the measurement point and the complete installation. This guide frames the questions a buyer can take into a sensor enquiry.

Begin with the question the measurement must answer. Is the point a furnace zone, pipe surface, vessel, bearing, winding, food-processing point or equipment test location? Record normal and upset conditions, the required response, the stability objective, available mounting space, vibration, process connection, cable route and receiving instrument. These details describe the duty more accurately than choosing a sensor name first. Also identify whether the reading is for control, indication, testing or equipment protection, because that purpose affects how the point and signal should be reviewed.

A thermocouple may be the relevant family for furnace, surface, process or test measurements. Catalogue records include J and K options across several thermocouple constructions, with grounded or ungrounded junctions on selected products. The Thermocouple MI record lists J and K choices, grounded and ungrounded junctions, and SS304, SS316, SS310 or Inconel600 sheath options. Those options still need to be matched to the actual point. Cable type, dimensions and termination should be taken from the selected record rather than inferred from another assembly.

RTD selection also depends on physical form and interface. Catalogue records include ring-type RTDs, L-bend ring RTDs, conduit RTDs, weatherproof-head RTDs and RTDs for particular process uses. For rotating equipment, the ring-type and L-bend forms should be considered against the available mechanical fit. For a process point, review the assembly, connection and instrument input together rather than assuming that an RTD format fits every installation. The route, bend space and replacement access can be as important as the sensing element.

Ask where the sensing junction or element will sit, whether electrical isolation matters, how the cable will be routed, and what the instrument is configured to accept. Compare the intended response with the access and replacement method, and confirm the supported sheath, cable and dimensions from the selected catalogue record. Note the process connection or mounting detail, the number of channels and any space limits on the drawing. Share the measurement point, operating conditions, mounting and signal requirements when requesting a suitable catalogue-backed option. Include existing sensor details when replacing an assembly so the comparison starts from the actual installation. State the preferred replacement quantity and installation access in the enquiry.
