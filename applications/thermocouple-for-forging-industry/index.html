Thermocouple for Forging Industry

Temperature measurement for billet heating, furnace zones, die heating and downstream heat-treatment operations.

Introduction

Forging temperature influences material flow, press load, die life and final properties. The measurement system must survive scale, radiant heat, door cycling, vibration and handling around heavy equipment.

Furnace thermocouples control the heating environment; they do not automatically prove that every billet has reached the same internal temperature. Sensor placement and soak practice must be considered together.

Process Context

Batch and continuous forging furnaces experience repeated loading, door opening and temperature recovery. Scale and combustion products attack protection tubes, while long probes can be damaged by charge movement.

Die and tooling measurements involve lower ranges and tighter spaces. Heat-treatment stages may prioritize uniformity and recorded thermal cycles rather than the rapid heating needed before deformation.

Selection Guidance

Type K or N suits many forging-furnace zones. Higher-temperature or stability-critical points may justify Type R or S. Select protection material for atmosphere, scale abrasion and thermal shock.

Place control thermocouples where they represent the controlled zone without direct flame or billet impact. Add independent monitoring points when furnace uniformity or recovery time must be assessed.

Recommended Sensors

Heavy-duty Type K or N

General billet-heating and furnace-zone control with a robust metallic or ceramic protection system.

Type R or S assembly

High-temperature duties requiring noble-metal stability and suitable ceramic protection.

Compact die sensor

Mineral-insulated or surface construction for die blocks, tooling and localized heating.

Installation Considerations

Shield the sensing assembly from billet impact and loading equipment. Allow for thermal expansion, and support long protection tubes without over-constraining them.

Locate the junction away from direct burner flame unless flame temperature is the intended measurement. Inspect buildup and cracking during planned shutdowns and replace degraded seals or heads.

Key Benefits

  • Improves control of billet-heating and furnace-recovery cycles.
  • Reduces breakage from scale, impact and unsupported insertion.
  • Supports consistent forming conditions and downstream treatment.
  • Extends service life through atmosphere-specific protection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which thermocouple is commonly used in forging furnaces?

Type K and N are common for many zones. Type R or S may be chosen for higher temperatures or stability requirements.

Can a furnace thermocouple measure billet core temperature?

It measures its local furnace environment. Billet core temperature depends on material, size, heating rate and soak time and may require a separate method or validated heating model.

What damages forging-furnace protection tubes?

Scale impact, charge movement, thermal shock, chemical attack, poor support and direct flame exposure are common causes.

How should a long probe be supported?

Support must limit vibration and bending while allowing thermal expansion. The design depends on length, orientation, material and furnace access.
