Application Guide

RTD Sensors for Motors & Bearings

Technical insight on protecting critical rotating equipment using precision Resistance Temperature Detectors (RTDs).

Introduction

The unexpected failure of large rotating equipment—such as industrial motors, generators, pumps, and compressors—often results in significant operational downtime and costly repairs. Monitoring the temperature of critical components is one of the most effective methods for early fault detection.

Precision RTDs are the standard choice for this application, providing highly accurate, stable, and repeatable measurements that allow control systems to detect subtle thermal increases before catastrophic damage occurs.

Process Context

Excessive heat in a motor or generator can originate from electrical overloading, poor ventilation, or the degradation of winding insulation. In bearings, temperature spikes typically indicate lubrication failure, misalignment, or mechanical wear.

Because the operating temperatures of these components generally remain below 200°C, RTDs are perfectly suited to the task, offering significantly better accuracy and resolution than base-metal thermocouples in this range.

Selection Guidance

For bearing monitoring, compact, vibration-resistant Pt100 RTDs (often spring-loaded) are inserted directly into the bearing housing to maintain contact with the outer race.

For stator winding monitoring, specialized flat 'stick' RTDs are embedded between the winding coils during motor manufacture or rewinding. These sensors must possess high dielectric strength to prevent electrical tracking.

Recommended Sensors

Spring-Loaded Bearing RTDs

Compact RTDs designed for insertion into bearing housings, utilizing spring mechanisms to ensure contact despite vibration.

Stator Winding RTDs (Stick Sensors)

Thin, flat RTD assemblies insulated with high-dielectric materials (like epoxy glass or polyimide) for embedding in motor slots.

Miniature Embedment RTDs

Small footprint sensors designed for potting directly into tight spaces within smaller motors or gearboxes.

Installation

  • When installing bearing RTDs, the sensor tip should rest firmly against the bearing's outer race without exerting excessive force that could deform the sheath. A thermally conductive paste is occasionally used to improve response time, though spring-loading is generally sufficient.
  • Stator RTDs must be carefully positioned during the winding process. Care must be taken not to pinch or sharply bend the lead wires, and the sensor must be thoroughly void-free during the vacuum pressure impregnation (VPI) process.

Key Benefits

  • Early warning of lubrication failure or mechanical wear before bearing destruction.
  • Prevention of stator insulation breakdown by monitoring localized hot spots.
  • Enables predictive maintenance strategies, reducing unplanned equipment downtime.
  • Provides data to safely maximize motor output without exceeding thermal limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why use a Pt100 RTD instead of a thermocouple for a bearing?

A Pt100 RTD offers greater accuracy, stability, and resolution at the typical operating temperatures of bearings (usually under 150°C), making it easier to detect the subtle temperature trends indicative of wear.

What is a 3-wire RTD and why is it used?

A 3-wire RTD incorporates an extra lead wire that allows the measuring instrument to calculate and subtract the electrical resistance of the cable itself, ensuring accurate measurements over long cable runs.

Can bearing RTDs withstand high vibration?

Yes, industrial bearing RTDs are typically manufactured using mineral-insulated cable, which tightly packs the sensing element in powder (MgO), rendering it highly resistant to vibration damage.

How are stator RTDs protected from high electrical voltages?

They are encapsulated in materials with high dielectric strength, such as specialized epoxies or polyimide films, designed to withstand several kilovolts without breaking down.
