Temperature Sensors for Boiler, Power & Cogeneration

Boiler and power facilities have distinct furnace, flue-gas, steam, feedwater, turbine, bearing and auxiliary measurement points. Selection follows the point and instrument interface. Separate point descriptions make comparison easier.

Boiler temperature measurement asks different questions at each location. A furnace or flue-gas point may use a thermocouple, while feedwater, condensate or a bearing point may use an RTD. Steam-line and auxiliary points need their connection and mounting reviewed with the measurement objective. Turbine monitoring can also involve RTD and process-scanner products, depending on the existing system requirement.

The available options include Thermocouple MI, Thermocouple With Weatherproof Head, Thermocouple With BSTK Nipple Union, RTD With Weatherproof Head, Ring Type RTD, Ring Type RTD with L Bend and Process Scanner Temperature Scanner. Compare these products against the actual measurement point, existing assembly and receiving instrument rather than selecting one sensor construction for every duty across the plant.

A useful specification separates combustion or gas-path measurement from water, steam and equipment points. Note the location, existing connection, insertion or contact arrangement, cable route and instrument channel for each point. For turbine and bearing work, include the mechanical space and the existing sensor form. This gives the buyer a practical way to compare thermocouple, RTD and scanner products without treating the plant as one uniform duty. The boiler, power-plant, rotating-equipment, flue-gas and turbine-bearing routes provide related context for organizing these questions.

Control, indication and maintenance requirements also affect how the points should be grouped. A plant may need a direct sensor input at one location and a separate transmitter, indicator or scanner at another. Recording the signal expected by the existing system helps narrow the relevant instrument products and avoids treating the sensing element as the entire measurement loop.

For a project with several points, list each location separately and identify whether the request is a replacement or a new specification. Include any existing sensor name, drawing, connection detail and instrument channel. This allows the product options to be compared point by point and keeps furnace, flue-gas, steam, feedwater, turbine and bearing questions distinct.

For an enquiry, provide the measurement point, medium, mounting or process connection, operating conditions, available space, lead route, signal input and required documentation. Include whether the point is for process monitoring or equipment temperature measurement, plus an existing sensor description where relevant. Yog Electro Process can then help identify the relevant product for review while keeping the selection tied to project information.
